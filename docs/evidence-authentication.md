# Evidence authentication — how an AgentDFIR case proves it is what it says

> **Not legal advice.** This page explains the integrity mechanisms of the
> `.adfir` format and which facts they establish, and maps them to the facts a
> certification of a qualified person attests to. It does not claim any package
> is "admissible" or "compliant" — those are decisions for a court and for
> counsel in your jurisdiction. Consult counsel.

An AgentDFIR case is designed so that anyone — using AgentDFIR, or using only
standard tools — can confirm it has not changed since it was sealed, and can see
who collected it, when, and on whose authority.

## The mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it establishes | Check with AgentDFIR | Check with standard tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content addressing** — every artifact's `artifact_id` is the SHA-256 of its plaintext | each file is exactly the bytes that were copied | `agentdfir verify <pkg>` (re-hashes every blob) | `gunzip -c raw/<sha>.gz \| sha256sum` equals the `artifact_id` in `manifest.jsonl` |
| **`SHA256SUMS` seal** — covers the whole sealed zone | nothing in the sealed set changed | `agentdfir verify <pkg>` | `cd <pkg> && sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS` (macOS: `shasum -a 256 -c`) |
| **`SEAL.sig`** — ed25519 over the seal | the seal was signed by a specific key | `agentdfir verify <pkg>` reports VALID/INVALID and the key | the `public_key` and `signature` are in `SEAL.sig`; verify with `openssl`/`ssh-keygen` tooling against the SHA-256 of `SHA256SUMS` |
| **Archived seals** (`seals/SHA256SUMS.<n>`) | each earlier round's sealed state is preserved | `agentdfir verify` covers them | `sha256sum seals/SHA256SUMS.<n>` against `prev_seal_sha256` in `case.json` |
| **Anchors** (recorded in `$AGENTDFIR_HOME` outside the case) | the seal digest existed at seal time, out of the case's reach | the digest is printed at the end of each run | compare the printed digest to the anchor record copied into a custody package |
| **Hash-chained logs** (`collection.jsonl`, `chain-of-custody.jsonl`) | no record was edited, inserted, deleted or reordered | `agentdfir verify` checks both chains | each line's `prev` is the SHA-256 of the previous line's exact bytes |

A single changed byte anywhere in the sealed zone breaks `verify` and
`sha256sum -c`.

## US context — FRE 902(13) and 902(14)

US Federal Rules of Evidence **902(13)** and **902(14)** (both effective
2017-12-01) allow certain electronic records to be *self-authenticating* on the
written certification of a qualified person:

- **902(13)** — a record generated by an electronic process or system that
  produces an accurate result. The agent transcripts, settings and logs AgentDFIR
  collects are records produced by the agent software in the normal course of its
  operation.
- **902(14)** — data copied from an electronic device, storage medium or file,
  authenticated by a process of digital identification such as a hash. The
  content addressing and `SHA256SUMS` seal are exactly that process.

Self-authentication concerns whether a record *is what it claims to be*. It is
not a ruling on relevance, hearsay or ultimate admissibility, and the
certification is made and signed by a **qualified person**, never by the tool.

### Which AgentDFIR artifact maps to which attested fact

| The certification attests… | …supported by |
|---|---|
| the records were generated by an electronic process | agent transcripts/settings in `manifest.jsonl`, collector identity in `case.json` |
| they were copied without alteration | `artifact_id` = SHA-256 of plaintext; `stored_sha256` for the bytes on disk |
| the copy's integrity can be verified by a hash process | `SHA256SUMS`, `verify`, and `SEAL.sig` when signed |
| who collected them, when, and on what authority | `chain-of-custody.jsonl`, `case.json` (operator, timezone), `case authorize` records |

### Other jurisdictions

Other frameworks differ. **ISO/IEC 27037** gives guidance on the identification,
collection, acquisition and preservation of digital evidence and is widely
referenced internationally; UK and EU practice rest on their own rules and codes.
This page describes mechanisms, not a legal conclusion in any jurisdiction.

## Chain-of-custody package — `agentdfir export custody`

`export custody` produces a self-contained, human-readable bundle beside the case
(derived data; the sealed case is not modified):

```sh
agentdfir export custody CASE.adfir --out custody-bundle
agentdfir export custody CASE.adfir --out custody.zip --certify \
    --name "Dana Lee" --title "GCFA" --org "Acme CSIRT"
```

The bundle contains:

- `custody.md` / `custody.json` — every custody event in order (sealed log plus
  the post-seal activity log), each with actor, time, action, round and digests,
  and the verified state of both hash chains;
- `seals/` — copies of `SHA256SUMS*`, `SEAL.sig*`, the signer's public key, and
  the anchor records for this case;
- `verify.txt` — a full `verify` run at export time and the exact commands to
  re-run it with AgentDFIR and with standard tools;
- `certification-902-14.md` — the template below, pre-filled with case facts
  (digests, rounds, dates, tool version); the human fields stay as `[[ ]]`
  placeholders and the tool never signs;
- `MANIFEST.sha256` — the SHA-256 of every file in the bundle, so a tampered copy
  of a seal is caught by `sha256sum -c MANIFEST.sha256`.

## Who authorized the acquisition — `agentdfir case authorize`

```sh
agentdfir case authorize CASE.adfir --by "J. Doe, IR lead" \
    --basis "incident IR-7, CISO approval" --reference IR-7
agentdfir case log CASE.adfir          # the sealed custody log + the activity log
```

`case authorize` records who authorized the acquisition into the case's custody
activity log — a hash chain outside the seal, where each record names the seal
digest it was made against. The accountability report and the custody package
both show it. The `--authorization <ref>` flag on `collect`/`run` still records a
reference at collection time; `case authorize` adds the named, attributed record.
